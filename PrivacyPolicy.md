# Privacy Policy

**Oath, Privacy Policy**
Last updated: October 23, 2026

## 1. Who we are and what this covers
Oath ("Oath", "we", "us", "our") is an iOS app for focus, app blocking, habits and alarms. This policy explains what information the app collects, what stays on your phone, what leaves it, who it goes to and why, and what you can do about it. It applies to the Oath app, its widgets and extensions, and the email and support channels listed at the end.

Oath was previously published under the name "Stoic". Same app, same accounts, same policy.

The short version: almost everything you do in Oath is stored on your phone and never sent to us. What does leave your phone is listed below, line by line.

## 2. What stays on your phone
The following is stored only on your device (or in the private app group shared with Oath's own widgets and extensions). We cannot see it, and deleting the app deletes it.

- Your habits, routines, Winter Arc and day counts
- Your tasks, to-dos and brain dumps after they have been turned into tasks
- Your journal: every daily verdict and every line you wrote
- Your book library and your notes about books
- Your streak, credits, focus history and Locked-In settings
- Your alarms and their settings, including any custom alarm sound you record
- Your name and anything you told us about yourself during setup, used to personalise the app on the phone
- Your Ghost Feed settings, your daily limits, and your Instagram, TikTok and YouTube logins (see section 5)
- Which apps you have chosen to block (see section 4)

## 3. What leaves your phone

### 3.1 Your account
When you first open Oath, it creates an anonymous account: a random identifier with no name or email attached. You can use the whole app this way.

If you choose to sign in, we receive and store:
- **Sign in with Apple:** your Apple user identifier and the email you share with us, or Apple's private relay address if you choose to hide your email.
- **Sign in with Google:** your Google account identifier and email address.
- **Email and password:** your email address and a securely hashed password. We never store the password itself.

A signed-in account exists so that you can prove it is yours. Your progress itself (habits, streak, journal, library) is stored on your phone as described in section 2.

If you sign in with an email address, we may send you one welcome email. We do not send marketing email.

### 3.2 Your profile
If you fill in a display name, handle, short bio, Instagram username or profile photo, these are stored on our servers with your account. Oath does not currently show profiles, rankings or messaging to other users. If we launch features that make any profile information visible to others, this policy will say so first.

### 3.3 Product analytics
Oath records which screens you reach, which buttons you tap, and which multiple-choice answers you pick during setup, so that we can see where people get stuck and fix it. These events are sent to our own database under a random analytics identifier generated on your phone. They carry no name, email, device identifier or precise location, and they are not linked to your account.

### 3.4 Push notifications
If you turn notifications on, Oath stores a device push token with your account so a notification can reach your phone. The token says nothing about you. Most of Oath's reminders are scheduled locally on your phone and never involve our servers.

### 3.5 Ad measurement (App Tracking Transparency)
Oath includes the Meta (Facebook) SDK to measure whether people who install Oath from an ad actually keep using it. When you first use the app, iOS asks whether Oath may track you.

- **If you allow tracking,** the Meta SDK may receive your device's advertising identifier together with app install, open and purchase events, and match them to the ad you saw.
- **If you do not allow tracking,** the advertising identifier is not shared and Meta receives only aggregated, non-identifying event data as permitted by Apple's rules.

In both cases this data is used only to measure advertising. It is never used to show you ads inside Oath, to build a profile of you, or to decide what you see in the app. You can change your choice at any time in iOS Settings under Privacy & Security, then Tracking. Meta's handling of this data is governed by Meta's privacy policy.

### 3.6 Subscriptions
Oath+ purchases are made through Apple. We never see your payment card. We use Superwall to present paywalls and measure them, and Superwall receives your account identifier, your first name if you have given one, and events about which paywall you saw and whether you purchased. To stop fraud, the app sends Apple's signed purchase receipt to our server, where its signature is checked and the subscription is recorded against your account.

### 3.7 AI features (only when you use them)
Three features send a single piece of content to our server, which passes it to OpenAI to process, returns the result, and keeps nothing:

- **Scan a book:** the photo of the cover, so a model can read the title and author. We then look the book up in Google Books and Open Library for a cover image and page count. The photo is not stored.
- **Brain dump:** the text you typed or dictated, so it can be turned into a task list. Dictation itself happens on your phone through Apple's keyboard. No audio is sent to us.
- **Roadmap suggestions:** the goal you typed, so a model can suggest steps.

OpenAI processes this content under its API terms and does not use it to train its models. AI output can be wrong; it is a suggestion, not advice.

### 3.8 Ghost Feed reports
When you tap Report inside Ghost Feed, the app sends us a description of how the Instagram, TikTok or YouTube page was built (element names and short labels such as "Sponsored"), which of the three it was, and the version of the filtering rules in use, so we can fix those rules. It never includes your messages, captions, photos, videos, comments or who you follow. The app also downloads the current filtering rules for each of the three from our server; those requests carry no account information.

### 3.9 Weather
If you allow location access, the morning screen fetches local weather from Open-Meteo using a city-level location (accuracy of about one kilometre). Your location is sent to Open-Meteo for that request and is not stored by us. You can decline, and the weather line simply does not appear.

### 3.10 Support
If you write to us, we receive whatever you choose to send from your own mail app. The "I'm stuck" button pre-fills the app version, iOS version, device model and your day count so we can help faster. You can delete any of it before sending.

## 4. Screen Time and app blocking (Locked-In)
Oath blocks apps using Apple's Screen Time framework (Family Controls, Managed Settings and Device Activity). When you choose which apps to block, Apple gives Oath opaque tokens, not app names. Oath does not know which apps you selected or anything about your wider Screen Time history.

To show how long you spent in your blocked apps today, Apple tells Oath when your time in them, taken together, passes set marks (for example 15 or 30 minutes). Oath keeps that daily total, and the last 14 days of it, in the private app group on your phone, where the app and its widgets read it. It is a single number per day, not tied to any named app, and it is never sent to us.

The Screen Time page and its widget draw your usage inside a sealed view that Apple provides. Apple fills in those numbers itself; Oath cannot read them, and they never leave your phone.

The settings needed to run these features live in the private app group on your phone, and none of it is sent to us. Face ID, when you use it to confirm spending credits, is handled entirely by iOS; Oath never sees biometric data.

## 5. Ghost Feed (Instagram, TikTok and YouTube)
Ghost Feed loads the Instagram, TikTok and YouTube websites inside Oath and hides the parts you ask it to hide, such as Reels, Shorts, the For You feed, suggestions and ads. Your logins, cookies and sessions for each service are stored by iOS in a separate, isolated web store for each account, on your phone only. We never see your passwords, your messages, your feed, the videos you watch, or what you scroll past. Signing out of an account in Ghost Feed wipes that store.

The time you spend in each service and your daily limits are counted on your phone and stay there.

To notify you of new Instagram direct messages, Oath periodically checks your Instagram inbox page in the background using your own session and turns a new unread count into a local notification. Nothing from that check leaves your phone.

When you share an Instagram, TikTok or YouTube link to Oath from another app, the link is held briefly in the app group until Oath opens it, then discarded.

Instagram is a product of Meta Platforms, Inc. TikTok is a product of TikTok Ltd. and its affiliates. YouTube is a product of Google LLC. Oath is not affiliated with, endorsed by or sponsored by any of them, and your use of each service remains subject to that service's own terms and privacy policy.

## 6. Alarms and wake-up challenges
- **Camera (push-ups and other rep challenges):** Oath uses Apple's on-device Vision framework to count movements. Nothing is recorded, saved or sent anywhere.
- **Microphone and speech (shout challenge):** the app listens for the phrase you have to say. Recognition runs on your phone whenever your iPhone supports it. On devices that do not, Apple's speech service processes the audio under Apple's privacy policy. Oath stores no audio from challenges.
- **Motion (step challenge):** step counts come from Apple's Core Motion and are read only during the challenge.
- **Custom alarm sounds** you record are stored on your phone only.

## 7. Background activity
To keep a focus session running after you switch apps, Oath may play a silent audio stream. It also uses iOS background refresh to check Ghost Feed's inbox (section 5). Neither collects data.

## 9. How we use information
- To run the features you use and keep your account working
- To understand where people get stuck in the app and improve it
- To deliver notifications you turned on
- To process subscriptions and prevent fraud
- To measure whether our advertising works
- To answer support requests
- To keep the service secure and to meet legal obligations

Where the GDPR or UK GDPR applies, our legal bases are: performance of our contract with you (running the app and your account), your consent (tracking, location, notifications, camera, microphone, motion, and the optional sign-in), and our legitimate interests (security, fraud prevention, understanding and improving the app, measuring advertising where consent is not required).

## 10. Retention
- **Account and profile data:** kept until you delete your account.
- **Analytics events:** kept under the random identifier; they cannot be tied back to you.
- **Subscription records:** kept while the subscription is active and for as long as needed to handle disputes and meet accounting obligations.
- **AI inputs** (cover photos, brain dump text, goals): not stored after the response is returned.
- **Ghost Feed reports:** kept until the rules they describe are fixed, then periodically cleared.
- **On-device data:** kept until you delete it or uninstall the app.

## 11. Deleting your account
Settings, then Account, then Delete Account removes your account, profile, profile photo, push tokens and subscription record from our servers. Data on the phone is removed when you delete the app. Purchases are managed by Apple and are not affected; cancel a subscription in your Apple ID settings.

## 12. Your rights
Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, delete, export or restrict the use of your personal data, to object to processing, and to withdraw consent. You can delete your account in the app, change permissions in iOS Settings, and email us at the address below for anything else. We answer within 30 days. You may also complain to your local data protection authority.

- **EEA and UK:** rights under the GDPR and UK GDPR as above.
- **California:** rights under the CCPA and CPRA to know, delete and correct, and to opt out of sale or sharing. We do not sell personal data. Allowing tracking may count as "sharing" for cross-context behavioural advertising under California law; you can opt out in iOS Settings.
- **Israel:** rights under the Protection of Privacy Law, 5741-1981.

## 13. Children
Oath is not directed at children under 13, and you must be 13 or older to use it. We do not knowingly collect personal data from anyone under 13. If you believe a child has given us personal data, email us and we will delete it.

## 14. Security
Data in transit is encrypted with TLS. Server data is encrypted at rest. Sign-in tokens are kept in the iOS Keychain. Server functions require authentication and are rate-limited. No system is perfectly secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security.

## 15. International transfers
Our servers and providers are located in the United States and elsewhere outside your country. Where required, transfers rely on standard contractual clauses or an equivalent safeguard.

## 16. Changes
We will update this policy when the app changes. The date at the top tells you when. For material changes we will tell you in the app before they take effect.
