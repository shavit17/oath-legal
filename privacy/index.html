Privacy Policy

Oath, Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 2, 2026

1. Who we are and what this covers

Oath ("Oath", "we", "us", "our") is an iOS app for focus, recorded work sessions, friends, app blocking, habits and alarms, made by Shavit Brosh in Israel, who is the controller of the personal data described here. This policy explains what information the App collects, what stays on your phone, what leaves it, who can see it, who it goes to and why, how long we keep it, and what you can do about it. It applies to the Oath app, its widgets and extensions, our servers, and the email and support channels listed at the end.

Oath was previously published under the name "Stoic". Same app, same accounts, same policy.

The short version: your habits, tasks, journal, alarms, Screen Time data and unposted recordings stay on your phone. What you post goes to your friends. Your messages are end-to-end encrypted, so we cannot read them. Everything that leaves your phone is listed below.

2. What stays on your phone

The following is stored only on your device (or in the private app group shared with Oath's own widgets and extensions). We cannot see it, and deleting the App deletes it.

  • Session recordings you have not posted, including drafts, and the camera frames used to make them
  • Videos you bring in from your photo library, until you post them
  • Your habits, routines, Winter Arc and day counts
  • Your tasks, to-dos and brain dumps after they have been turned into tasks
  • Your journal: every daily verdict and every line you wrote
  • Your book library and your notes about books
  • Your credits, focus history and Locked-In settings
  • Your alarms and their settings, including any custom alarm sound you record
  • Your name and what you told us about yourself during setup, used to personalise the App on the phone
  • Your Ghost Feed settings, daily limits, and your Instagram, TikTok and YouTube logins (see section 6)
  • Which apps you have chosen to block (see section 5)
  • The private key that decrypts your messages, in the iOS Keychain

3. What leaves your phone

3.1 Your account

When you first open Oath, it creates an anonymous account: a random identifier with no name or email attached.

If you choose to sign in, we receive and store:

  • Sign in with Apple: your Apple user identifier and the email you share with us, or Apple's private relay address if you choose to hide your email.
  • Sign in with Google: your Google account identifier and email address.
  • Email and password: your email address and a securely hashed password. We never store the password itself. Our database provider sends the emails needed to confirm or reset it.

We do not send marketing email.

3.2 Your profile, and what other users can see

To use the Feed, friends and messages, you choose a username. Your profile card is visible to any signed-in Oath user who searches for you or opens your page. It shows:

  • your display name, username and profile photo;
  • your bio, if you add one;
  • your current streak, your number of friends, and the total and longest time of the sessions you have posted.

If you add an Instagram username to your profile, it is stored with your account but not shown on your profile card.

Other users can find you by searching for your name or username. Your sessions, stats breakdowns and posts are visible only to your friends; to everyone else your page says it is private. People you have blocked cannot find you or see your page.

3.3 Sessions and posts

Sessions you keep to yourself ("Only me") and drafts stay on your phone and are never uploaded. When you post a session to your friends, we store, on our servers:

  • the clip (the timelapse you recorded or the video you brought in, after your edits), its cover picture and its preview frame;
  • the session's details: when it started and ended, its length, its tags, your note, the habit it counted toward, the song preview you chose, the people you tagged, and any text, stickers or drawing you added;
  • who can see it.

Clips are kept in private storage and are only served, through short-lived links, to the people allowed to see the post. Face blurring and other edits are applied on your phone before upload. A session's times show your friends when you were working.

Your friends can give your posts kudos and comment on them. Kudos (with the names of who gave them) and comments are visible to anyone who can see the post. Comments are not end-to-end encrypted.

3.4 Friends, live status and nudges

We store your friend requests, friendships, nudges, mutes, blocks, per-friend notification settings and your activity list (kudos, comments, requests). While you record a session, your friends can see that you are recording now, the tag you chose and when you started; this ends when the session ends. Weekly totals and rankings among friends are worked out from the sessions you have posted.

3.5 Messages

Messages (text, photos, voice notes and files) are end-to-end encrypted on your phone before they are sent, using a key that never leaves your device. Our servers store only the encrypted message, which we cannot read, together with what is needed to deliver it: the conversation, the sender, the time, the type of message and its size. We also store your public encryption key so others can write to you, and, for group chats, the group's name and members. If you lose your phone and its key, past messages cannot be recovered.

3.6 Push notifications

If you turn notifications on, Oath stores your device's push token with your account. Notifications about other users' actions (a message, kudos, a comment, a friend request, a nudge, a friend starting or posting a session) are sent through Apple's Push Notification service. A message notification carries only the sender's name; a comment notification carries the start of the comment. Most of Oath's own reminders are scheduled locally on your phone.

3.7 Music

When you search for a song, the search words are sent to Apple's iTunes Search service, which returns songs and 30-second previews. Previews are streamed from Apple. The song you choose (its title, artist, artwork link and identifiers) is saved with your session.

3.8 Sharing and invites

When you share a clip or an invite, Oath creates the file or text on your phone and hands it to the app you pick (for example Messages, WhatsApp or Instagram) or saves it to Photos if you ask. What happens to it there is governed by that app. Oath does not access your contacts.

3.9 Reports and safety

When you report a post or a person, we store who reported, what was reported and the reason, and we review it. We may keep reports, and the content they concern, for as long as needed to keep users safe, to handle repeat abuse and to meet legal obligations.

3.10 Product analytics

Oath records which screens you reach, which buttons you tap, and which multiple-choice answers you pick during setup, so that we can see where people get stuck and fix it. These events are sent to our own database under a random analytics identifier generated on your phone. They carry no name, email, device identifier or precise location, and are not linked to your account.

3.11 Ad measurement (App Tracking Transparency)

Oath includes the Meta (Facebook) SDK to measure whether people who install Oath from an ad keep using it. iOS asks whether Oath may track you.

  • If you allow tracking, the Meta SDK may receive your device's advertising identifier together with app install, open and purchase events, and match them to the ad you saw.
  • If you do not allow tracking, the advertising identifier is not shared and Meta receives only aggregated, non-identifying event data as permitted by Apple's rules.

This data is used only to measure advertising. It is never used to show you ads inside Oath. You can change your choice at any time in iOS Settings under Privacy & Security, then Tracking.

3.12 Subscriptions

Oath+ purchases are made through Apple. We never see your payment card. We use Superwall to present and measure paywalls; it receives your account identifier, your first name if you have given one, and events about which paywall you saw and whether you purchased. To stop fraud, the App sends Apple's signed purchase record to our server, where its signature is checked and the subscription is recorded against your account.

3.13 AI features (only when you use them)

Two features send a single piece of content to our server, which passes it to OpenAI, returns the result, and keeps nothing:

  • Scan a book: the photo of the cover, so a model can read the title and author. We then look the book up in Google Books and Open Library for a cover image and page count.
  • Brain dump: the text you typed or dictated, so it can be turned into a task list. Dictation happens on your phone through Apple's keyboard; no audio is sent to us.

OpenAI processes this content under its API terms and does not use it to train its models. We count how many AI requests each account makes, to limit abuse.

3.14 Ghost Feed reports and rules

When you tap Report inside Ghost Feed, the App sends us a description of how the Instagram, TikTok or YouTube page was built (element names and short labels such as "Sponsored"), which service it was, and the rules version in use, so we can fix the filtering. It never includes your messages, captions, photos, videos, comments or who you follow. The App also downloads the current filtering rules; those requests carry no account information.

3.15 Weather

If you allow location access, the morning screen fetches local weather from Open-Meteo using a city-level location (about one kilometre). The location goes to Open-Meteo for that request and is not stored by us.

3.16 Support

If you write to us, we receive whatever you choose to send from your own mail app. The "I'm stuck" button pre-fills the App version, iOS version, device model and your day count; you can delete any of it before sending.

4. Camera, microphone and photos

  • Recording sessions: the camera captures frames on your phone to make the timelapse. Nothing is sent unless you post the session.
  • Videos from your library: you pick a video through Apple's photo picker. Oath receives only the video you pick and cannot see the rest of your library.
  • Saving to Photos: Oath adds a clip to your library only when you ask, and needs permission only to add.
  • Voice notes in messages are recorded with the microphone and sent end-to-end encrypted.
  • Wake-up challenges: the camera counts exercise with Apple's on-device Vision framework, and the microphone listens for the phrase you must say. Speech recognition runs on your phone where supported; otherwise Apple processes the audio under Apple's privacy policy. Nothing from a challenge is stored or sent by us. Step counts come from Apple's Core Motion during the challenge only.

5. Screen Time and app blocking (Locked-In)

Oath blocks apps using Apple's Screen Time framework. When you choose apps to block, Apple gives Oath opaque tokens, not app names, so Oath does not know which apps you chose or your wider Screen Time history. Apple tells Oath when your time in your blocked apps, taken together, passes set marks; Oath keeps that daily total for 14 days on your phone, and it is never sent to us. The Screen Time page and widget show your usage in a sealed view that Apple fills in and Oath cannot read. Face ID, when used to confirm spending credits, is handled entirely by iOS.

6. Ghost Feed (Instagram, TikTok and YouTube)

Ghost Feed loads the Instagram, TikTok and YouTube websites inside Oath and hides the parts you ask it to hide. Your logins, cookies and sessions for each service are stored by iOS in a separate, isolated web store on your phone only. We never see your passwords, messages, feed or what you watch. Signing out of an account in Ghost Feed wipes its store. Time spent and daily limits are counted on your phone. To tell you about new Instagram direct messages, Oath may check your Instagram inbox page in the background using your own session and turn a new unread count into a local notification; nothing from that check leaves your phone. Your use of each service remains subject to its own terms and privacy policy.

8. How we use information

  • To run the features you use and keep your account working
  • To show your posts, profile and activity to the people allowed to see them, and to deliver messages and notifications
  • To keep users safe: to review reports, enforce our Terms and Community Rules, and stop spam, fraud and abuse
  • To understand where people get stuck in the App and improve it
  • To process subscriptions and prevent fraud
  • To measure whether our advertising works
  • To answer support requests and meet legal obligations

Where the GDPR or UK GDPR applies, our legal bases are: performance of our contract with you (running the App, your account, your posts, friends and messages), your consent (tracking, location, notifications, camera, microphone, photo library, motion and the optional sign-in), our legitimate interests (safety and moderation, security, fraud prevention, understanding and improving the App, and measuring advertising where consent is not required), and legal obligation.

9. Retention

  • Account and profile: until you delete your account.
  • Posts, clips, covers, comments and kudos: until you delete them or your account. A deleted post can be undone for a few seconds, then it and its files are removed.
  • Messages: the encrypted messages you sent are removed when you delete your account; messages others sent you stay with them.
  • Friendships, requests, nudges, blocks and settings: until you or the other person changes them, or you delete your account.
  • Live status: only while you are recording.
  • Reports: as long as needed for safety and legal purposes, as described in section 3.9.
  • Analytics events: kept under the random identifier; they cannot be tied back to you.
  • Subscription records: while the subscription is active and as long as needed to handle disputes and meet accounting obligations.
  • AI inputs: not stored after the response is returned.
  • Ghost Feed reports: until the rules they describe are fixed, then cleared.
  • Backups of our database are kept for a limited period and then overwritten.
  • On-device data: until you delete it or uninstall the App.

10. Deleting your account

Settings, then Account, then Delete Account removes, from our servers, your account, profile and profile photo, your posts and their clips and pictures, your comments and kudos, your friendships, requests and nudges, the messages you sent, your live status, push tokens and subscription record. Data on the phone is removed when you delete the App. Copies other people saved or shared before you deleted cannot be recalled by us. Purchases are managed by Apple; cancel a subscription in your Apple ID settings.

11. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, delete, export or restrict the use of your personal data, to object to processing, and to withdraw consent at any time without affecting earlier processing. You can edit your profile and delete posts and your account in the App, change permissions in iOS Settings, and email us for anything else. We answer within 30 days and may need to verify that the request comes from you. You may also complain to your local data protection authority.

  • EEA and UK: rights under the GDPR and UK GDPR as above.
  • California and other US states with privacy laws: rights to know, access, delete and correct, and to opt out of sale, sharing or targeted advertising. We do not sell personal data. Allowing tracking may count as "sharing" for cross-context behavioural advertising under California law; you can opt out in iOS Settings. We will not discriminate against you for using these rights.
  • Israel: rights under the Protection of Privacy Law, 5741-1981, and its regulations.

12. Children

Oath is not directed at children under 13, and you must be 13 or older to use it. We do not knowingly collect personal data from anyone under 13. Where the law of your country requires a parent's consent for a teenager to use online services, that consent is required. If you believe a child under 13 has given us personal data, email us and we will delete it.

13. Security

Messages are end-to-end encrypted. Data in transit is encrypted with TLS, and server data is encrypted at rest. Each user can reach only their own data and what others have chosen to share with them, enforced by row-level security on our database. Clips are in private storage served through short-lived links. Sign-in tokens and your message key are kept in the iOS Keychain. Server functions require authentication and are rate-limited. No system is perfectly secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security. If a breach affects your personal data, we will tell you and the authorities as the law requires.

14. International transfers

Our servers and providers are located in the United States and elsewhere outside your country. Where required, transfers rely on standard contractual clauses or an equivalent safeguard.

15. Changes

We will update this policy when the App changes. The date at the top tells you when. For material changes we will tell you in the App before they take effect.
