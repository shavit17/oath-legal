# Copyright and DMCA Policy

**Oath, Copyright and DMCA Policy**
Last updated: November 2, 2026

## 1. Our position
Oath respects the intellectual property of others and expects its users to do the same. Users can post content to Oath, including session clips, videos brought in from their photo library, cover pictures, photos, drawings, captions, comments and profile details. You may only post content that you own or have permission to use. We respond to clear notices of alleged copyright infringement as described in this policy and in line with the United States Digital Millennium Copyright Act ("DMCA"), 17 U.S.C. § 512, and we terminate the accounts of repeat infringers.

This policy is part of our Terms of Service.

## 2. Reporting infringement (takedown notice)
If you believe content available through Oath infringes a copyright you own or are authorised to act for, send a written notice to our Designated Copyright Agent (section 7). To be effective under the DMCA, the notice must include:

1. A physical or electronic signature of a person authorised to act on behalf of the owner of the copyright that is allegedly infringed.
2. Identification of the copyrighted work claimed to have been infringed, or, if multiple works are covered by one notice, a representative list of them.
3. Identification of the material claimed to be infringing and information reasonably sufficient for us to locate it. In Oath, the most useful details are the username (handle) of the account that posted it, the date it was posted, and a description of the post, comment or profile item. A screenshot helps.
4. Information reasonably sufficient for us to contact you, such as your name, address, telephone number and email address.
5. A statement that you have a good faith belief that use of the material in the manner complained of is not authorised by the copyright owner, its agent or the law.
6. A statement that the information in the notice is accurate, and, under penalty of perjury, that you are authorised to act on behalf of the owner of the right that is allegedly infringed.

Notices that leave out these elements may not be acted on. Please do not use this process for disputes that are not about copyright, such as privacy, harassment or trademark concerns; report those in the App or by email, and we will handle them under our Terms of Service and Community Rules.

## 3. What we do with a notice
When we receive a notice that substantially complies with section 2, we will, without undue delay:
- remove or disable access to the material identified;
- notify the user who posted it, and give them a copy of the notice (with your personal contact details removed where the law allows, other than what the DMCA requires us to share); and
- record the notice against that user's account for our repeat infringer policy.

Messages in Oath are end-to-end encrypted, and we cannot see their contents. We cannot review or remove the contents of a private message, but we can act on the account that sent it when we receive a valid notice that identifies it.

## 4. Counter-notice
If your content was removed because of a notice and you believe the removal was a mistake or a misidentification, or that you have the right to use the material, you may send a counter-notice to our Designated Copyright Agent. To be effective, it must include:

1. Your physical or electronic signature.
2. Identification of the material that was removed or to which access was disabled, and where it appeared before it was removed.
3. A statement, under penalty of perjury, that you have a good faith belief that the material was removed or disabled as a result of mistake or misidentification of the material.
4. Your name, address and telephone number, and a statement that you consent to the jurisdiction of the Federal District Court for the judicial district in which your address is located (or, if your address is outside the United States, any judicial district in which we may be found), and that you will accept service of process from the person who provided the original notice or an agent of that person.

When we receive a valid counter-notice, we will forward it to the person who sent the original notice. If they do not tell us, within 10 business days, that they have filed a court action seeking to restrain you from the infringing activity, we may restore the material within 10 to 14 business days after receiving the counter-notice.

## 5. Repeat infringers
We will terminate, in appropriate circumstances, the accounts of users who are repeat infringers. As a rule, an account that receives three valid, unresolved copyright notices within twelve months is terminated, and we may terminate sooner for flagrant or deliberate infringement. A terminated user may not create a new account.

## 6. Misrepresentation
Under 17 U.S.C. § 512(f), any person who knowingly and materially misrepresents that material is infringing, or that it was removed or disabled by mistake or misidentification, may be liable for damages, including costs and attorneys' fees. Think before you send a notice or a counter-notice. If you are not sure whether something infringes, consider getting legal advice first.

Rights holders with concerns about a song in a post can use the process above, and may also contact Apple about its preview service.
